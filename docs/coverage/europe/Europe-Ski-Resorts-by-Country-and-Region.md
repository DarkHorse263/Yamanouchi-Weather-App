# feelzlike Europe: ski resorts by country and region

Publish-ready version, 9 Oct 2026. There are 154 new resorts in 86 new regions across 16 countries, plus Lech Zürs and St Anton, which are already live.

Every resort has been checked for:

- a confirmed 2026-27 winter season, with a source
- a resort-specific base and top height (not the whole linked network's)
- an exact map point with its terrain height
- an official website

Companion files: `europe_ski_resorts.csv` (all fields and source links) and the Replit build pack.

## How to read it

- One row per resort or sector with its own base village. Linked lift networks such as Les 3 Vallées, Portes du Soleil and Dolomiti Superski are shown as a region with separate resort pages. A resort is never credited with the whole network's heights (the Lech Zürs rule).
- **Snow mid:** the base–top midpoint, where snow will be forecast.
- **Village:** the terrain height at the village map point, used for village weather.
- **Fixed:** shows where this round replaced a network-wide or conflicting figure.

## Notes on sources

- 56 season confirmations come from the resort or lift company's own site. 35 come from skiresort.info. The rest come from regional tourism boards, operator-affiliated sites or ski-industry date listings, all with specific 2026-27 dates. Planned dates can still move with snow.
- Heights come from official sites where they publish them. Otherwise they come from skiresort.info, Wikipedia or established ski guides. The link for each resort is in the CSV.
- Glacier and valley-gondola resorts (Pitztal, Kaunertal, Stubai, Axamer Lizum, Sainte-Foy, Saint-Lary, Jenner, Nevis Range) have their village point below the lift base. This is intentional: village weather stays in the town, and the snow forecast uses the mountain midpoint.
- Not in this pass: small local hills, Georgia, Türkiye, Iceland, Greece, indoor slopes, and heli-only operations.
