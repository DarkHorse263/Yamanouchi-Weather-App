These are tables of ski resorts in [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria "Austria"), sorted by [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province "Province").

As a country dominated by the [Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alps "Alps"), Austria offers many opportunities for [alpine skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_skiing "Alpine skiing"). Alpine skiing is a major driver of tourism in Austria, and therefore an important economic factor.

In particular, the provinces of [Vorarlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorarlberg "Vorarlberg"), [Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrol "Tyrol") and [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg_(state) "Salzburg (state)") offer one or more ski resorts in almost every Alpine valley, equipped with numerous lifts (cable cars, [chair lifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairlift "Chairlift"), [T-bar lifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_lift "Surface lift")). The large ski areas in the Austrian Alps have 50 or more different lifts and sometimes more than 100km of groomed slopes. However, these are often not connected and can only be reached by bus. The [Ski amadé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_Amad%C3%A9 "Ski Amadé") network in the province of Salzburg, for example, has 860 kilometres of pistes and 270 lifts, but they are spread over 25 villages in an area of several hundred square kilometres.

However, there are also many ski resorts in the eastern provinces. The smallest of these often have only one or two T-bar lifts and a few hundred metres of pistes.

Ski resorts are often combined with other winter sports facilities, such as cross-country trails, toboggan runs or thermal spas.

There are also some [glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier "Glacier") ski resorts in Austria, which essentially offer year-round winter sports regardless of snow conditions.

Some of Austria's ski resorts repeatedly host [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_cup "World cup") races.
