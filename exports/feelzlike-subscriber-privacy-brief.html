subscriber information & privacy

How feelzlike handles signups, and what to resolve before advertising.

Assessment: safeguards exist, but worldwide compliance is not yet verified.
This is a source-code and regulatory guidance review, not a production security test or legal sign-off. Existing public policies need corrections. Do not describe the service as “fully compliant” or “100% secure”.

1 · what subscribers provide

Free powder alerts: email, chosen regions/resorts, snowfall threshold, forecast window, delivery preference and timezone. Confirmation, unsubscribe and delivery-history records are also stored. Optional push alerts add a browser delivery endpoint and keys.

Accounts: Clerk manages sign-in; the app stores a linked identity, email and profile/preferences, such as display name, units and home region. No live payment checkout was established by this review. Paid plans require a separate billing/privacy review before activation.

2 · where it goes & safeguards

Application records go to the configured PostgreSQL database; Clerk handles identity; the email implementation defaults to Resend. Analytics/advertising and diagnostics may involve Google, Meta and Sentry. Exact production vendors, locations, retention and processor contracts still need verification.

Code includes explicit alert consent, email confirmation, signed management links, one-click unsubscribe and authenticated account routes. Meta is designed to require advertising consent. Replit documents encryption in transit and at rest; that does not independently prove this deployment’s configuration or access controls.

3 · privacy policy first, terms second

Update the Privacy Policy and short signup notice with collected data, purposes/legal bases, named processors, overseas transfers, retention, rights and contact details. Link the notice at collection.

Align the T&Cs with free alerts, account rules, service limitations and unsubscribe. Add price, renewal, cancellation and refund terms only before paid subscriptions launch. Acceptance of terms is not permission for unrelated marketing.

4 · actions before scaling ads

  • Verify production account isolation, stable alert signing configuration, deletion completeness and outstanding legacy-data access concerns.
  • Correct retention claims: unsubscribing stops messages but currently retains alert records. Set and enforce a justified retention/suppression schedule.
  • Reconcile analytics notices with GA denied-mode pings, first-party cookieless counts and Sentry diagnostics/replay. “Cookieless” does not automatically mean consent-exempt.
  • Confirm legal entity/contact details, vendor agreements and transfer safeguards. Establish access/deletion request and breach-response procedures; restrict staff access and use MFA.
  • Keep promotional consent separate from weather-alert consent; retain evidence of the wording/version and choice. Do not upload subscriber emails to ad audiences without a separate lawful assessment.

5 · which countries’ rules matter?

Scope depends on the business, people targeted and tracking performed, not just where the ski resort is. Free subscriptions can still be covered. Have counsel confirm the actual ad markets.

AustraliaSpam Act consent, sender identity and unsubscribe for commercial messages. Privacy Act / APP scope depends on turnover and exceptions; assess overseas disclosures.
New ZealandPrivacy Act and commercial-message rules where applicable: collection notice, security, rights, overseas safeguards and unsubscribe.
JapanAssess APPI scope, specified purposes, foreign-transfer requirements and promotional email consent rules.
CanadaAssess PIPEDA/provincial laws, including Quebec, and CASL. Commercial email generally needs consent evidence, identification and unsubscribe.
United StatesCAN-SPAM for commercial email; state privacy-law thresholds vary. Assess advertising “sale/sharing”, applicable opt-outs/GPC and children’s privacy obligations.
EU / AustriaTargeting EU users or monitoring their behaviour can trigger GDPR, even for a free service. Assess notices, lawful bases, nonessential tracking consent, transfers and EU representative requirements.
General information, not legal advice. Owner/internal use; not a replacement for the public Privacy Policy. UK and Switzerland need separate scope checks if targeted.
